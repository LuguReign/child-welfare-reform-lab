Quality decision log

Exact duplicates are removed. Invalid dates, orphan references and overlapping care episodes are quarantined. Conflicting duplicate keys quarantine all versions. Unknown values are not silently imputed.

Quarantining can change eligibility and create selection bias. Resolve exclusions with the source agency before using production results.

Limits that affect interpretation

The public workbook is a single release, not a historical reform series. Indicators refer to different cohorts. RSP is copied from the federal source; rebuilding it requires the source microdata and federal methodology. Federal benchmarks do not substitute for court-approved measure definitions. Synthetic trends are generated examples and support no substantive policy claims.

The visit denominator excludes partial months. Missing visits count as not documented; absent records cannot distinguish a missed visit from incomplete recording. Real use requires agreements on data completeness, face-to-face definitions, exception handling, disclosure control and reviewer sign-off.

Questions for a monitoring team

  1. Which settlement provision and approved measure specification govern each indicator?
  2. Are source extracts complete, frozen and reconciled to the agency inventory?
  3. Which exclusion or missing-data patterns could alter findings?
  4. What do children, families and frontline staff identify as barriers behind the numbers?
  5. Who owns each correction, by when, and what evidence closes the issue?
